The formula

One beat, a quarter note in 4/4, lasts 60,000 ÷ BPM milliseconds. At 128 BPM that's 468.75 ms. Halve it for an 1/8 note, halve again for a 1/16, and multiply by four for a whole bar.

What to use it for

Delay. Set the delay time to an 1/8 or a dotted 1/8 and the echoes land on the grid instead of smearing it. Most DAW delays can sync to tempo, but plugins that only take milliseconds need the number.

Reverb pre-delay and decay. A pre-delay of a 1/32 or 1/64 note keeps the dry hit clear. A decay that ends before the next beat keeps the mix from washing out.

Sidechain release. A release around a 1/16 or 1/8 note lets the bass come back before the next kick. Too short and it clicks, too long and the bass never recovers on fast patterns.

Kick and 808 timing. A few milliseconds matter in the low end: an 808 that starts 10 ms late can cancel a kick on the same note. See Kick and 808 phase.

See your timing in milliseconds

Smartscope locks to your project's tempo, from a 1/16 note up to 4 bars. Zoom in and the grid switches to milliseconds, so you can see exactly where the kick and the bass start. It's free, for Windows.
