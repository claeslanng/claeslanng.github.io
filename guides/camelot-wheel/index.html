Camelot wheel

Keys that sit next to each other on the wheel mix without clashing: the same code, one number up or down, or the other ring with the same number. Click a key to see its matches.

The wheel

Click a key on the wheel

The keys that mix well with it light up. Outer ring: major keys (B). Inner ring: minor keys (A).

    The three safe moves

    Every key gets a number from 1 to 12 and a letter: A for minor, B for major. Neighbors on the wheel share all but one note, so they mix without clashing.

    Same code. 8A to 8A: the same key. Always safe.

    One step around. 8A to 7A or 9A: up or down a fifth. Smooth, and keeps the mood.

    Switch rings. 8A to 8B: A minor to C major, the relative key. Same notes, brighter or darker mood.

    For an energy lift

    Seven steps around the wheel, for example 8A to 3A, moves the key up one semitone. It isn't a smooth blend, but it's a well-known trick to lift a set at a drop or a quick cut.

    For producers too

    The same rules help when you pick samples: a loop in 8A works over chords in 8A, 7A, 9A or 8B. Check the key of every sample before you build on it.
