Bass in mono

Wide bass usually comes from making the left and right sides different. In mono they're added together, and the parts that point opposite ways cancel. Keep the low end in mono and check your mix in mono.

Try it

A bass note on A1 (55 Hz), made wide with the oldest trick there is: the right side plays a few milliseconds late. Listen in stereo, then in mono.

Why it happens

In mono, the left and right channels are simply added together. When both sides play the same thing at the same time, that just makes it louder. When one side is delayed or flipped, some frequencies arrive with their waves pointing the opposite way, and those cancel.

A delay of a few milliseconds cancels a whole row of frequencies, evenly spaced, starting at one over twice the delay. At 9.1 ms that's 55 Hz, right on the bass note, then 165 Hz, 275 Hz and up. Flipping one side's polarity is worse: in mono everything the two sides share disappears.

Where people hear your track in mono

More often than you'd think: a lot of club systems run the subwoofers in mono, and many phones, Bluetooth speakers and smart speakers play through a single speaker. A bass that's huge in your headphones can be thin or gone on all of those.

How to keep it

Width is fine higher up. The low end is where it hurts.

Keep the lows in mono
Keep the sub and the body of the bass in the center. If you want width, add it only above the low end, for example to the top of the bass or a layer an octave up.
Check in mono often
Put a mono switch or a utility plugin on the master and flip it while you mix. If the bass drops, find out which plugin is making it wide.
Watch stereo wideners and chorus
Wideners, chorus and short stereo delays all work by making the sides different. On a bass, they're the usual cause.
Use a widener that checks mono for you
A widener with a mono guard pulls the width back on its own in any band that would cancel out. That's what MonoSafe does.

Width that survives mono

MonoSafe is a free four-band stereo widener with a built-in guard: it automatically pulls back the width in any band that would cancel out or lose level in mono. It's a free Flow plugin and runs in Flow FMX Host on Windows.
